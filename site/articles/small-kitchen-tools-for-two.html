The Right-Sized Kitchen: Pots, Pans, and Tools for Cooking for Two

By the Just Two Plates team · Updated November 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Big stockpots and 9 by 13-inch pans are built for families. When you are cooking for two, smaller equipment heats faster, cooks more evenly, and is easier to lift and wash. Here is what we use most.

Pans and pots

  • An 8- or 10-inch nonstick skillet for eggs, pancakes, and quick skillet dinners.
  • A 10-inch cast-iron or stainless skillet that can go into the oven for searing steak or pork tenderloin.
  • A 2- to 3-quart saucepan with a lid, perfect for soups for two.
  • A 3- to 4-quart pot for pasta.

For the oven

  • A quarter-sheet pan (about 9 by 13 inches) for sheet-pan dinners for two.
  • An 8-inch square baking dish for small casseroles and crisps.
  • Four 6- to 8-ounce ramekins for molten chocolate cakes, apple crisp, and individual servings.
  • Two oven-safe soup bowls for mini pot pies.

Helpful tools

  • An instant-read thermometer takes the guesswork out of cooking meat safely.
  • A small food processor or chopper for quick chopping.
  • A kitchen scale for measuring small amounts of meat or pasta.
  • Freezer-safe containers in one- and two-serving sizes.
  • Lightweight pans with comfortable handles, which are easier on wrists than heavy cast iron for everyday cooking.

Comfort and safety

Choose tools with large, easy-to-grip handles, and keep everyday items on lower shelves within easy reach. Silicone oven mitts that cover the forearm are a worthwhile upgrade.
